Demand Forecasting with Machine Learning

Keywords: time series, demand forecasting, machine learning.

  • View code on Kaggle

Project Overview

In this project, I explored the application of machine learning to retail demand forecasting in a highly volatile and data-rich environment. Using the Online Retail dataset, I transformed raw transactional data into structured time series and developed predictive models to estimate product-level demand. The analysis followed the CRISP-DM framework and focused on comparing Random Forest and XGBoost, assessing not only their predictive performance but also their robustness in the presence of sparse, irregular, and intermittent demand patterns.

Approach and Findings

A key part of the work involved extensive data preparation and feature engineering, including the removal of cancellations and outliers, and the creation of temporal, lag-based, and rolling features to capture seasonality and demand dynamics. While XGBoost showed slightly higher accuracy in initial tests, it demonstrated instability and sensitivity to data noise, whereas Random Forest delivered more consistent and balanced predictions. These findings reinforced the importance of data quality, feature design, and bias control over model complexity alone.

Business Relevance

Although the current solution should be considered a prototype, the project highlights the strong potential of machine learning to support retail decision-making, particularly when combined with richer features such as promotions, pricing, and external factors. In a real-world setting, this approach could be scaled within a modern data architecture to improve inventory planning, reduce costs, and enhance service levels.
